05 / UNDERSTAND THE DATA

What the numbers mean.

Added uptake and total carbon

Added uptake excludes carbon already present in untreated water. Total dissolved inorganic carbon includes that baseline and is expressed here as CO₂ equivalent. Both concentrations come from the supplied PHREEQC results.

Dissolution efficiency

Additional dissolved CO₂ mass divided by cylinder mass loss, multiplied by 100. Setting values are arithmetic means of the individual efficiencies. The 30 Hz calculation uses an assumed water collection of 8 kg.

Repeat spread

Error bars show one sample standard deviation above and below the mean. They describe variation between individual trials, rather than full measurement uncertainty. Individual trials are available for every speed.

Water delivery

Water collections were independently weighed at 20, 40 and 50 Hz. At 30 Hz, the water mass is assumed at 8 kg per collection. That assumption does not verify delivery accuracy or water-mass repeatability.

At 20 and 50 Hz, there are two trials at 5 L/min and three at every other setting. At 30 Hz, there are three trials at 5 L/min and two at every other setting. At 40 Hz, there are two trials at each setting.

The 20 and 30 Hz records use the supplied trial tables. The 40 Hz records use the corrected table. The 50 Hz records use the original workbook. All setting averages are recalculated from individual trials, so small differences from previously rounded summaries may occur.

The 30 Hz water-mass assumption

Collected water was not independently recorded on the scale during the 30 Hz trial series. An 8 kg water collection is therefore assumed for each trial, matching the configured setpoint.

This assumption is used to calculate dissolved CO₂ masses and dissolution efficiency. It does not change the supplied PHREEQC uptake concentration.

A zero difference between the assumed mass and setpoint is not presented as measured delivery accuracy. Likewise, identical assumed masses are not evidence of measured repeatability.

Why the 20 Hz water-delivery issue matters

The water setpoint stayed at 10 kg, but collected water mass fell as CO₂ supply increased. At the 25 L/min CO₂ setting, average collected water was approximately 7.71 kg.

Heavy bubbling was observed. Gas interference in the downstream flowmeter and changes in hydraulic conditions are possible explanations. Their contributions have not been confirmed by these trials.

The downstream meter controls batch shut-off, so its registered volume should be checked against independently weighed collections. The meters also disagreed at lower gas settings; their difference cannot be attributed entirely to increasing gas supply.

Calculations and interpretation limits
  • Supplied CO₂ mass is cylinder weight loss in kilograms multiplied by 1,000.
  • Water mass is converted to approximate volume using a density of 1 kg/L.
  • Additional dissolved CO₂ mass is uptake concentration multiplied by the collection's water volume.
  • Total dissolved carbon mass is total concentration multiplied by the collection's water volume, expressed as CO₂ equivalent.
  • Dissolved masses are calculated for each individual trial before setting averages are calculated.
  • Mean dissolution efficiency is the arithmetic mean of individual trial efficiencies.
  • PHREEQC concentrations depend on the supplied water chemistry and measured conditions.
  • Small cylinder weight changes are sensitive to weighing resolution.
  • Point 1 and Point 2 readings remain as recorded. Their average is not treated as independently verified water flow.
  • Recorded pressure values are retained, but their reference and measurement location are not confirmed here.
  • System pH is kept separate from handheld pH. Recorded system dissolved-CO₂ values are not treated as calibrated reference measurements.
  • CO₂ not accounted for as dissolved uptake cannot automatically be classified entirely as vented gas.
  • Collection timestamps are not collection durations. Delivery rates per minute are not inferred from them.
  • Uptake targets are user-defined. No universal good-performance threshold is assigned.
  • A target comparison shows an arithmetic difference, without assigning a measurement tolerance.
