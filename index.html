03 / BATCH DOSING

From water uptake to cement dose.

Use the selected operating point’s added CO₂ concentration to calculate the CO₂ carried into a batch by carbonated water.

Added CO₂ dose relative to cement

Dose (%) = uptake (g/L) × carbonated water (L) ÷ cement mass (kg) ÷ 10. The calculation assumes the selected concentration is retained until mixing. It represents added CO₂ delivered through water. Mineralized CO₂ requires a separate measurement. Native carbon in untreated water is excluded.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE TESTS

The method, without three reports.

What changed?

Pump speeds of 30, 40 and 50 Hz were investigated. At each speed, CO₂ flowmeter settings were 5, 10, 15, 17, 20 and 25 L/min. Actual water flow, pressure and temperature were recorded responses.

What is uptake?

PHREEQC-derived added CO₂ concentration, based on the supplied water chemistry and measured conditions. Multiplying added concentration by collected water volume gives the added CO₂ mass retained in that water.

What is efficiency?

Added CO₂ mass in collected water divided by cylinder mass loss, multiplied by 100. Flowmeter settings identify test conditions. Cylinder mass loss provides the supplied CO₂ mass used in the efficiency calculation.

What do error bars show?

One sample standard deviation above and below the mean. This describes variation between repeats. It does not represent full measurement uncertainty. At 50 Hz, there were two repeats at 5 L/min and three at each other setting.

Sources and limits of this summary

The 50 Hz results and repeat statistics come from the supplied 50HZ.xlsx workbook. The 30 and 40 Hz values are summary means from the earlier analysis. Their individual repeats and standard deviations are not included in this version.

Collected water mass is converted to approximate volume using a density of 1 kg/L. Water chemistry, temperature, pressure, flow and measurement conditions should be considered when comparing separate test series. Small cylinder mass changes are sensitive to weighing resolution.

This page presents tested settings. It does not predict performance at untested conditions.

Why investigate 50 Hz for Flamingo?

The wider question is whether two parallel Venturi–static mixer branches at 50 Hz can approach the dissolution performance of one pair at 20 Hz while increasing water throughput.

That comparison is pending. Water throughput, added uptake, gas consumption and efficiency need to be assessed together.
